# Quizzes

Short, self-marking checks. A quiz here is **not** a test of a whole level; it is a
five-to-ten minute diagnostic used *before* study (to find gaps) or *one week after*
(to check retention).

---

## 1. Quiz format

Every quiz page follows this shape:

```text
Q1. [question]
    A. [option]
    B. [option]
    C. [option]
    D. [option]

Answer: B
Why: [one or two sentences explaining the correct choice and the most attractive
wrong choice]
```

The "Why" line is the part that teaches. A quiz without explanations is a scoreboard,
not a study tool.

## 3. How to use a quiz score

| Score | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 18–20 / 20 | Secure | Move on; revisit in a month |
| 15–17 | Mostly secure with holes | Reread the chapters behind the missed items |
| 11–14 | Fragile | Rework the level's exercise bank |
| ≤ 10 | Not yet learned | Restart the level with the study routine |

Do not use quiz scores to compare yourself with anyone. They exist to route your next
hour of study.

## 4. Writing a quiz question

Good chemistry multiple-choice items have a **distractor built from a real
misconception**, not a random wrong number. For example:

> The pH of 0.01 mol dm⁻³ $\ce{HCl}$ is:
> A. 1 · B. 2 · C. 12 · D. 0.01
>
> **Answer:** B. **Why:** a strong monoprotic acid fully dissociates, so
> $[\ce{H+}] = 0.01$ mol dm⁻³ and $\mathrm{pH} = -\log(0.01) = 2$. Option A comes from
> forgetting the logarithm; option C from computing pOH; option D from confusing the
> concentration with the pH.
