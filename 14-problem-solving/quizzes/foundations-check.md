# Foundations Check

> **Level 0 · 20 questions · 15 minutes**
> **Covers:** chapters 1–10 of [Level 0 — Foundations](../../01-foundations/README.md).
> **Use:** take it cold before you start Level 0, or one week after you finish it.

Answer all twenty questions before looking at any answer. Write your choices on a

---

**Q1.** Which of these changes is a chemical change?

- A. Ice melting at 0 °C
- B. Sugar dissolving in water
- C. Iron rusting in damp air
- D. Water boiling at 100 °C

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** Rusting produces hydrated iron(III) oxide, a new substance with different properties. Melting, dissolving and boiling change only the state or the degree of dispersion. Option B is the attractive distractor: the sugar seems to disappear, but evaporating the water recovers it unchanged, so no new substance has formed.

</details>

---

**Q2.** Which branch of chemistry is defined by the study of carbon compounds?

- A. Inorganic chemistry
- B. Organic chemistry
- C. Analytical chemistry
- D. Physical chemistry

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Organic chemistry is the chemistry of carbon compounds, with a few traditional exclusions such as carbon monoxide, carbon dioxide and the carbonate ion. Option A is tempting because "inorganic" sounds like "not from living things", but the modern definition is structural, not biological.

</details>

---

**Q3.** Which of the following is a pure substance?

- A. Air
- B. Brass
- C. Sodium chloride
- D. Sea water

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** Sodium chloride has a fixed composition and a single type of particle arrangement, so it is a compound and therefore a pure substance. Air and sea water are mixtures whose composition varies with place. Option B is the attractive distractor: brass looks uniform, but it is an alloy of copper and zinc, and its composition can be varied.

</details>

---

**Q4.** Which mixture is homogeneous?

- A. Sand stirred into water
- B. Oil shaken with water
- C. Salt dissolved in water
- D. Iron filings mixed with sulfur powder

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** A homogeneous mixture has a single phase with uniform composition throughout. Sand settles, oil forms a separate layer, and the iron–sulfur mixture is visibly heterogeneous. Option A is the common error: stirring makes a mixture look uniform for a moment, but the phases separate again.

</details>

---

**Q5.** A compound differs from a mixture in that a compound:

- A. can be separated by filtration
- B. has components in a fixed ratio by mass
- C. contains only one type of atom
- D. is always a liquid at room temperature

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** The law of definite proportions is the defining feature: a compound has a fixed composition, and its elements can be separated only by chemical means. Option C confuses a compound with an element — a compound contains two or more elements chemically combined.

</details>

---

**Q6.** Which state of matter has a definite volume but no definite shape?

- A. Solid
- B. Liquid
- C. Gas
- D. Plasma

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Particles in a liquid are close together but free to move past one another, so the volume is fixed while the shape follows the container. Option A keeps both a definite shape and a definite volume; gases and plasmas have neither.

</details>

---

**Q7.** Plasma is best described as:

- A. a gas of neutral molecules only
- B. an ionised gas containing free electrons and ions
- C. a liquid cooled below its freezing point
- D. a solid in which electrons are free to move

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** A plasma is a gas in which a significant fraction of the atoms or molecules are ionised, so it contains free electrons and ions and conducts electricity. Option D describes a metal, not a plasma; the difference is that a metal is a condensed solid, not an ionised gas.

</details>

---

**Q8.** Water boils at 100 °C at 1 atm. While it is boiling, the temperature stays at 100 °C because:

- A. no heat is being added to the water
- B. the heat is used to overcome intermolecular attractions rather than to raise kinetic energy
- C. water has an unusually high specific heat capacity
- D. the thermometer has reached thermal equilibrium

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** During a phase change the added energy increases potential energy, not average kinetic energy, so the temperature stays constant until all the liquid has vaporised. Option C is attractive because water's high specific heat is real, but specific heat explains slow heating, not the plateau.

</details>

---

**Q9.** Which of these is a physical change?

- A. Burning magnesium ribbon
- B. Baking a cake
- C. Magnetising an iron nail
- D. Milk turning sour

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** Magnetising changes the alignment of magnetic domains but not the identity of the iron. Burning, baking and souring all produce new substances. Option A is the most common error: the bright flame is dramatic, but drama is not the criterion — the formation of a new substance is.

</details>

---

**Q10.** Which observation is the most reliable evidence that a chemical change has occurred?

- A. A colour change
- B. A new substance with different properties has formed
- C. Bubbles appear
- D. The temperature changes

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** A chemical change is defined by the formation of a new substance, so the direct evidence is a change in identity and properties. Colour changes, bubbles and temperature changes can all be physical: dilution changes colour, boiling produces bubbles, and dissolving can cool a solution.

</details>

---

**Q11.** 10.0 g of calcium carbonate is heated and decomposes completely into 5.6 g of calcium oxide and carbon dioxide. What mass of carbon dioxide is produced?

- A. 4.4 g
- B. 5.6 g
- C. 15.6 g
- D. 0 g

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** By conservation of mass, the mass of the products equals the mass of the reactant: $10.0 - 5.6 = 4.4$ g. Option C adds the masses instead of subtracting them, which would create matter; option B simply repeats the mass of the other product.

</details>

---

**Q12.** Which of the following is an element?

- A. Carbon dioxide
- B. Oxygen gas
- C. Air
- D. Water

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Oxygen gas consists of molecules containing only oxygen atoms, so it is an element. Air is a mixture, and carbon dioxide and water are compounds. Option C is the attractive distractor: air contains oxygen, but it also contains nitrogen, argon, carbon dioxide and water vapour.

</details>

---

**Q13.** Which of the following is a compound?

- A. $\ce{O2}$
- B. $\ce{N2}$
- C. $\ce{NaCl}$
- D. Brass

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** Sodium chloride contains two elements chemically combined in a fixed ratio, which is the definition of a compound. Options A and B are elements, and brass is a mixture. Option D catches students who assume that a solid with a uniform appearance must be a compound.

</details>

---

**Q14.** An ion is formed when an atom:

- A. gains or loses electrons
- B. gains or loses protons
- C. shares electrons with another atom
- D. gains or loses neutrons

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** Removing or adding electrons changes the charge without changing the element, which is exactly what an ion is. Option B is the classic misconception: changing the proton count changes the element itself, producing a different atom rather than an ion of the same element.

</details>

---

**Q15.** Which subatomic particle carries a negative charge and occupies the space outside the nucleus?

- A. Proton
- B. Neutron
- C. Electron
- D. Nucleon

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** The electron carries a charge of $-1$ and is bound in the region around the nucleus. Option D is a category error: "nucleon" is a collective name for protons and neutrons, both of which are found in the nucleus.

</details>

---

**Q16.** In the periodic table, a vertical column is called a:

- A. period
- B. group
- C. block
- D. shell

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Vertical columns are groups, and elements in the same group share the number of valence electrons and therefore similar chemistry. Option A is the standard confusion: periods are the horizontal rows.

</details>

---

**Q17.** How many atoms are represented in one formula unit of $\ce{Ca(OH)2}$?

- A. 3
- B. 4
- C. 5
- D. 6

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** The formula contains one calcium, two oxygen and two hydrogen atoms: $1 + 2 + 2 = 5$. Option A counts the three symbols and ignores the subscript and the bracket; option B counts one oxygen and two hydrogens together with the calcium.

</details>

---

**Q18.** What is the correct formula for aluminium oxide?

- A. $\ce{AlO}$
- B. $\ce{Al2O3}$
- C. $\ce{Al3O2}$
- D. $\ce{AlO2}$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Aluminium forms $\ce{Al^3+}$ and oxygen forms $\ce{O^2-}$; the lowest common multiple of the charges is 6, so two aluminium ions balance three oxide ions. Option C swaps the subscripts, which would require $\ce{Al^2+}$ and $\ce{O^3-}$, ions that these elements do not form.

</details>

---

**Q19.** How many significant figures are in the measurement 0.00420 m?

- A. 2
- B. 3
- C. 4
- D. 6

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** The leading zeros are placeholders and are not significant; the digits 4, 2 and the trailing 0 after the decimal point are, giving three. Option A ignores the trailing zero, which is significant because it is written after the decimal point.

</details>

---

**Q20.** When diluting concentrated sulfuric acid, you should:

- A. pour water into the acid
- B. add the acid slowly to the water with stirring
- C. pour both into the same beaker at the same time
- D. warm the acid first

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Dilution is strongly exothermic, so the acid must be added to a larger volume of water, where the heat is absorbed safely. Option A is the dangerous misconception: adding water to concentrated acid can boil the surface layer and spatter acid out of the vessel.

</details>
