# Organic Check

> **Level 4 · 20 questions · 25 minutes**
> **Covers:** chapters 1–14 of [Level 4 — Organic Chemistry](../../05-organic-chemistry/README.md).
> **Use:** at the end of Level 4, or before starting the inorganic and physical levels.

Organic questions reward precision about *where* something happens. Before choosing an
option, say the mechanism to yourself: which atom is the nucleophile, which is the
electrophile, and which face of the molecule is accessible. Most wrong answers here are
mechanistically impossible rather than merely unlikely.

---

**Q1.** Which functional group defines ethanoic acid, $\ce{CH3COOH}$?

- A. Alcohol
- B. Aldehyde
- C. Ketone
- D. Carboxylic acid

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D.** The carboxyl group, $-\ce{COOH}$, contains a carbonyl and a hydroxyl on the same carbon and is the highest-priority group present, so the compound is named as an acid. Option C catches students who see the C=O and ignore the OH on the same carbon; the two groups together are not a ketone.

</details>

---

**Q2.** What is the IUPAC name of $\ce{CH3CH(CH3)CH2OH}$?

- A. 2-methylpropan-1-ol
- B. 2-methylpropan-2-ol
- C. butan-1-ol
- D. 2-methylbutan-1-ol

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** The longest chain containing the OH is three carbons, the OH is on carbon 1, and the methyl branch is on carbon 2. Option B puts the OH on the middle carbon, which would require the OH to be attached to a carbon bearing two methyl groups; option C ignores the branch.

</details>

---

**Q3.** How many stereocentres does 2-bromobutane contain?

- A. 0
- B. 1
- C. 2
- D. 3

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Carbon 2 carries Br, H, a methyl group and an ethyl group — four different substituents — so it is a stereocentre. The other carbons carry at least two identical hydrogens. Option C is the common overcount, produced by treating the CH2 carbon as chiral when it has two hydrogens.

</details>

---

**Q4.** At a stereocentre, priority 4 points away from you and the sequence 1 → 2 → 3 runs clockwise. What is the configuration?

- A. R
- B. S
- C. E
- D. Z

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** With the lowest-priority group pointing away, the observed sense is read directly: clockwise is R. Option B applies the reversal rule that is needed only when priority 4 points towards you. Options C and D describe alkene geometry, not tetrahedral configuration.

</details>

---

**Q5.** In a Fischer projection, the horizontal bonds:

- A. point away from the viewer
- B. point towards the viewer
- C. lie in the plane of the paper
- D. represent hydrogen bonds

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** The convention is that vertical bonds recede and horizontal bonds project towards the viewer, which is why a 90° rotation in the plane inverts every stereocentre. Option A reverses the convention and is the single most common cause of a wrong R/S assignment from a Fischer drawing.

</details>

---

**Q6.** Which conformation of butane about the C2–C3 bond is lowest in energy?

- A. Anti-periplanar
- B. Gauche
- C. Eclipsed
- D. Syn-periplanar

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** The anti conformer has the two methyl groups at 180°, maximising their separation and minimising steric repulsion. Option B is a local minimum, but the methyl groups are only 60° apart and the conformer lies about 3.8 $\mathrm{kJ\ mol^{-1}}$ higher.

</details>

---

**Q7.** Which step is the initiation step in the radical chlorination of methane?

- A. $\ce{Cl2 -> 2Cl.}$ under ultraviolet light
- B. $\ce{CH4 + Cl. -> CH3. + HCl}$
- C. $\ce{CH3. + Cl2 -> CH3Cl + Cl.}$
- D. $\ce{CH3. + Cl. -> CH3Cl}$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** Initiation is the step that creates radicals from non-radical starting materials, here by homolysis of the Cl–Cl bond. Options B and C are the two propagation steps, which consume one radical and produce another; option D is a termination step, which removes radicals.

</details>

---

**Q8.** What is the major product of the addition of $\ce{HBr}$ to propene?

- A. 1-bromopropane
- B. 2-bromopropane
- C. 1,2-dibromopropane
- D. Propane

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Markovnikov addition puts the hydrogen on the carbon with more hydrogens, because that route passes through the more stable secondary carbocation. Option A is the anti-Markovnikov product, which requires a radical mechanism with peroxides, not the ionic addition of HBr.

</details>

---

**Q9.** Which order correctly ranks carbocation stability?

- A. Tertiary > secondary > primary > methyl
- B. Methyl > primary > secondary > tertiary
- C. Primary > secondary > tertiary > methyl
- D. Secondary > tertiary > methyl > primary

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** Alkyl groups stabilise the positive charge by induction and hyperconjugation, so stability rises with substitution. Option B inverts the trend and is the usual result of confusing carbocation stability with carbanion stability, where the order is reversed.

</details>

**Q10.** Why is benzene aromatic?

- A. It has six π electrons in a planar, cyclic, fully conjugated system, satisfying 4n + 2 with n = 1
- B. It has three alternating double bonds, which is the definition of aromaticity
- C. All six carbons are sp³ hybridised
- D. It reacts rapidly with electrophiles, and rapid reaction is the test for aromaticity

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** Aromaticity requires a cyclic, planar, fully conjugated system with 4n + 2 π electrons; benzene has six, so n = 1. Option B is the common misconception: drawing three double bonds does not make a compound aromatic, and cyclo-octatetraene has four double bonds yet is not aromatic because it is non-planar.

</details>

---

**Q11.** Which substituent directs electrophilic substitution to the meta position?

- A. $-\ce{OH}$
- B. $-\ce{NH2}$
- C. $-\ce{CH3}$
- D. $-\ce{NO2}$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D.** The nitro group withdraws electron density by both resonance and induction, destabilising the ortho and para intermediates and leaving the meta positions relatively favoured. Options A, B and C are all electron-donating and therefore ortho/para directing.

</details>

---

**Q12.** What is the rate law for an SN2 reaction?

- A. $\text{rate} = k[\text{substrate}][\text{nucleophile}]$
- B. $\text{rate} = k[\text{substrate}]$
- C. $\text{rate} = k[\text{nucleophile}]$
- D. $\text{rate} = k[\text{substrate}]^2$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** SN2 is a single concerted step in which the nucleophile attacks as the leaving group departs, so both species appear in the rate law. Option B is the SN1 rate law, and it is the most attractive distractor because students often learn "SN1 is first order" without learning why the nucleophile is absent from the slow step.

</details>

---

**Q13.** A chiral secondary haloalkane reacts by an SN1 mechanism. What happens to the stereochemistry?

- A. Complete inversion
- B. Complete retention
- C. Racemisation, often with a slight excess of inversion
- D. No reaction occurs

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** The planar carbocation can be attacked from either face, so the product is racemic; a slight excess of inversion arises because the departing group partially shields one face. Option A describes SN2, where backside attack is the only pathway.

</details>

---

**Q14.** An E2 elimination requires:

- A. a syn-periplanar arrangement of the β hydrogen and the leaving group
- B. an anti-periplanar arrangement of the β hydrogen and the leaving group
- C. no base
- D. a carbocation intermediate

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** The anti-periplanar geometry allows the developing p orbitals to overlap as the C–H and C–X bonds break together in one concerted step. Option D describes E1, which proceeds through a carbocation and is not concerted.

</details>

---

**Q15.** Oxidation of a primary alcohol with pyridinium chlorochromate gives:

- A. a carboxylic acid
- B. an aldehyde
- C. a ketone
- D. an alkene

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** PCC is a mild oxidant that stops at the aldehyde; distillation removes the product before it can be oxidised further. Option A would require a stronger oxidant such as acidified dichromate under reflux, and option C is impossible from a primary alcohol because the carbon already carries two hydrogens.

</details>

---

**Q16.** In the nucleophilic addition to a carbonyl compound, the nucleophile attacks:

- A. the carbonyl oxygen
- B. the carbonyl carbon
- C. the α-carbon
- D. the alkyl substituent

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** The carbonyl carbon is electrophilic because the C=O bond is polarised towards oxygen, and it is the site of attack. Option A reverses the polarity: the oxygen is electron-rich and is attacked only by electrophiles, as in protonation.

</details>

---

**Q17.** Which order correctly ranks the reactivity of carboxylic acid derivatives towards nucleophilic acyl substitution?

- A. Acid chloride > anhydride > ester > amide
- B. Amide > ester > anhydride > acid chloride
- C. Ester > acid chloride > amide > anhydride
- D. Anhydride > amide > acid chloride > ester

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** Reactivity falls as the leaving group becomes a weaker base and as the lone pair on the heteroatom donates into the carbonyl, both of which apply most strongly to the amide. Option B is the exact reverse and is the usual error of assuming that the most stable compound must be the most reactive.

</details>

---

**Q18.** Hydrolysis of an ester with aqueous sodium hydroxide gives:

- A. a carboxylic acid and an alcohol
- B. a carboxylate salt and an alcohol
- C. an acid chloride and an alcohol
- D. an amide and an alcohol

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Hydroxide attacks the ester and the alkoxide leaves, but the product is deprotonated by the basic medium, so the isolated organic product is the carboxylate salt. Option A describes acid-catalysed hydrolysis, where the carboxylic acid is obtained directly.

</details>

---

**Q19.** A Grignard reagent reacts with formaldehyde. What class of alcohol is produced?

- A. Primary
- B. Secondary
- C. Tertiary
- D. No alcohol forms

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** Formaldehyde has two hydrogens on the carbonyl carbon, so addition of one alkyl group gives $\ce{RCH2OH}$, a primary alcohol. Option B would require an aldehyde with one alkyl group, and option C a ketone with two.

</details>

---

**Q20.** The Diels–Alder reaction between a diene and a dienophile is:

- A. a [2+2] photochemical cycloaddition
- B. a [4+2] thermal cycloaddition giving a cyclohexene
- C. a nucleophilic substitution
- D. an elimination followed by addition

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Four π electrons from the diene and two from the dienophile combine in a concerted thermal cycloaddition to form a six-membered ring with one double bond. Option A is the common confusion with the photochemical [2+2] cycloaddition of two alkenes, which is a different reaction with a different stereochemical outcome.

</details>
