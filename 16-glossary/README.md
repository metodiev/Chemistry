## Why a glossary matters in chemistry

Chemistry has three overlapping naming systems — systematic IUPAC names, long-standing
trivial names, and historical names embedded in industrial and medical usage — and
they do not always agree. "Acetic acid" and "ethanoic acid" are the same substance;
"acetone" and "propan-2-one" are the same substance; "muriatic acid" and
"hydrochloric acid" are the same substance. A glossary that records the mapping, the
preferred name and the first chapter where the term appears removes a large class of
avoidable confusion.

## Conventions used in the glossary

- **Preferred term first**, in bold, followed by accepted alternatives in
  parentheses: **ethanoic acid** (acetic acid).
- **Symbols** are given where a term has one, with units.
- **First appearance** links to the chapter that defines the term properly.
- **Obsolete or discouraged terms** are marked *(obsolete)* or *(discouraged)* rather
  than omitted, because readers will meet them in the literature.
