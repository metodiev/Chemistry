# References and Data Sources

This file lists the sources the repository relies on. Every chapter's "Further
Reading" section cites from this list, and every quantitative constant traces back to
one of the primary data sources below.

**Policy.** Only sources that can be verified are listed. Where a chapter gives a
numerical value, it names the source in the sentence or the table caption. No DOI,
page number or edition is invented. If you find a citation that does not resolve,
please open a `correction` issue.

## 1. Authoritative data and recommendations

### 1.1 IUPAC

- International Union of Pure and Applied Chemistry. *Compendium of Chemical
  Terminology* ("Gold Book"), 2nd ed. (the "corrected" version).
  <https://goldbook.iupac.org/>
- IUPAC. *Nomenclature of Organic Chemistry: IUPAC Recommendations and Preferred
  Names 2013* (the "Blue Book"). Royal Society of Chemistry, 2014.
- IUPAC. *Nomenclature of Inorganic Chemistry: IUPAC Recommendations 2005* (the
  "Red Book"). Royal Society of Chemistry, 2005.
- IUPAC. *Quantities, Units and Symbols in Physical Chemistry* (the "Green Book"),
  3rd ed. Royal Society of Chemistry, 2007.
- IUPAC. *Compendium of Analytical Nomenclature* (the "Orange Book"), 3rd ed.
  <https://publications.iupac.org/orangebook/>
- IUPAC Commission on Isotopic Abundances and Atomic Weights. "Atomic weights of the
  elements" (standard atomic weights, biennial review).
  <https://ciaaw.org/atomic-weights.htm>
- IUPAC. *Biochemical Nomenclature and Related Documents* (the "White Book" and
  enzyme nomenclature, EC numbers). <https://www.qmul.ac.uk/sbcs/iubmb/enzyme/>

- National Institute of Standards and Technology. *CODATA Internationally

  <https://physics.nist.gov/cuu/Constants/>

### Notes on the 2019 SI redefinition

Since 20 May 2019 the base units are defined by fixed numerical values of $h$, $e$,
$k_\mathrm{B}$, $N_\mathrm{A}$, $c$ and $\Delta\nu_\mathrm{Cs}$. As a result
$N_\mathrm{A}$ and $k_\mathrm{B}$ are now *exact*, and the molar gas constant
$R = N_\mathrm{A} k_\mathrm{B}$ is exact. The kilogram is realised through the Kibble
balance rather than the international prototype.

### Standard-state conventions

- **IUPAC** standard pressure is $p^\circ = 10^{5}$ Pa (1 bar). Older tables often
  use 1 atm = 101 325 Pa; the difference changes $\Delta G^\circ$ by less than
  1 kJ mol⁻¹ for typical reactions, but should be stated.
- Standard concentration is $c^\circ = 1$ mol dm⁻³.
- Biological standard state is pH 7, denoted $\Delta G^{\circ\prime}$.
